Questions, answered.

What is OpenAlt?

OpenAlt is a free, open source app that turns an Android phone or tablet into a second monitor, a mirror of a Linux PC, a game screen or a desk panel called the Dock. You install it on the PC and on the device, then pair them by scanning one QR code.

Is OpenAlt free?

Yes. OpenAlt is licensed under GPL-3.0, with no ads, no account and no subscription. The downloads and the source code of every version are on GitHub.

Which computers does it work on?

Linux PCs with GNOME on X11 today: an AppImage for any distribution and a .deb for Ubuntu, Debian, Zorin and Pop!_OS. Windows is next; Wayland comes after that.

Which phones and tablets work?

Any phone or tablet with Android 5 or newer, standing or lying down, placed on any side of any monitor.

What is the Dock mode?

The Dock, new in version 0.2.0, turns a phone or tablet into a desk panel beside your monitor: music with cover and synced lyrics, volume up to 150% and audio devices, shortcuts and your PC’s apps, processor and graphics card temperature, the weather, and a big clock when nothing plays.

Does the Dock stream video?

No. The PC only sends data (the song, the volume, the temperatures) over your home network, so it doesn’t load the graphics card. It works only on the local network and only with devices you paired.

Can I use it away from home?

Yes, to mirror your PC over 4G, 5G or any Wi-Fi, through your own Tailscale account, with no router port to open. The Dock works only at home.

Is it safe?

Pairing uses a single-use QR code that expires in two minutes, with no PIN. The PC only accepts devices you paired, and forgetting a device cuts it off from the stream and the Dock at once. Nothing about you is sent anywhere.

Take your PC anywhere.

Install on the PC, get the app on the phone, scan once. Empowering anyone to stream anywhere.
